INDUSTRIAL INDUCTION MOTOR MAINTENANCE SOP
==========================================

SYSTEM PURPOSE:
This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) provides engineering guidance for
industrial squirrel-cage induction motor defects identified through infrared
radiometric thermography, electrical signature telemetry, and condition monitoring.

Monitored Conditions:
1. Shaft Misalignment (MISALIGNMENT)
2. Broken Rotor Bars (BROKEN_ROTOR)
3. Normal Motor Operation (MOTOR_NORMAL)


==================================================
1. SHAFT MISALIGNMENT (MISALIGNMENT)
==================================================

Fault Code:
MISALIGNMENT

Fault Description:
Geometric axis deviation between the motor drive shaft and driven machine shaft
(parallel offset, angular deflection, or combination misalignment).

Severity:
HIGH

Possible Root Causes:
- Thermal growth differential between motor and driven machine feet during load.
- Soft foot condition (uneven contact between motor feet and base frame).
- Inaccurate initial mechanical alignment during installation.
- Pipe strain, ductwork forces, or dynamic coupling degradation.
- Shifting foundation bolts or mechanical settling over operational cycles.

Typical Symptoms:
- Pronounced left-to-right axial thermal gradient across the coupling face.
- Temperature rise concentrated on drive-end bearing housing and flexible coupling.
- Elevated 1X and 2X rotational frequency vibration in radial and axial planes.
- Accelerated bearing raceway edge loading and coupling insert wear.

Inspection Procedure:
1. Measure coupling temperature and record axial thermal differential across coupling halves.
2. Check for soft foot using a dial indicator or precision laser alignment gauge (max allowable 0.05 mm).
3. Perform laser shaft alignment measuring both vertical and horizontal parallel offset and angular gap.
4. Inspect flexible coupling insert/elastomer spider for shredding, wear particles, or thermal melting.
5. Verify torque specifications on motor hold-down bolts.

Immediate Safety Action:
1. If coupling temperature exceeds 80°C or vibration RMS exceeds ISO 10816-3 Zone C limits, initiate controlled ramp-down.
2. Tag out and lock out (LOTO) motor drive supply prior to opening coupling guards.
3. Allow coupling to cool to ambient temperature before disassembling coupling bolts.

Corrective Maintenance:
1. Loosen motor hold-down bolts and clean shim packs beneath all feet.
2. Correct soft foot using precision stainless steel shims.
3. Align shafts using reverse indicator method or dual-laser alignment tool within 0.05 mm offset and 0.04 mm/100 mm angular tolerance.
4. Replace damaged flexible coupling elements.
5. Re-torque hold-down bolts in criss-cross pattern to specified Nm values.

Preventive Maintenance:
1. Conduct monthly thermal imaging scans of coupling and drive-end bearing.
2. Perform vibration spectrum check for 2X harmonic peaks during scheduled PM intervals.
3. Recalculate cold-to-hot thermal alignment offsets after major operating cycle changes.


==================================================
2. BROKEN ROTOR BARS (BROKEN_ROTOR)
==================================================

Fault Code:
BROKEN_ROTOR

Fault Description:
Cracking, partial separation, or complete rupture of squirrel-cage rotor conductor
bars or end-rings due to cyclic mechanical and thermal fatigue.

Severity:
CRITICAL

Possible Root Causes:
- Frequent direct-on-line (DOL) starts causing severe thermal expansion and bar mechanical stress.
- Cyclic magnetic forces from high-inertia mechanical loads.
- Casting porosity, air voids, or manufacturing defects in die-cast aluminum/copper rotor cages.
- Persistent motor overloading and excessive operating temperatures.
- Rotor bar vibration in rotor laminations due to loose bar fit.

Typical Symptoms:
- Localized thermal hotspots along the motor frame corresponding to rotor bar positions.
- Sideband frequencies around electrical supply fundamental (f_b = f_L * (1 ± 2s)) in vibration and current spectra.
- Fluctuating motor current, cyclic rotational rumble, and torque ripple under load.
- Reduced starting torque and extended acceleration times.

Inspection Procedure:
1. Measure localized frame temperature using thermal imaging to identify non-uniform stator heating patterns.
2. Conduct Motor Current Signature Analysis (MCSA) to measure decibel difference between fundamental line frequency (50/60 Hz) and pole-pass sidebands.
3. Perform a single-phase rotor rotation test (measuring current variation as rotor is manually turned).
4. Perform core flux leakage testing if rotor is pulled during teardown.
5. Inspect end-ring brazed joints and bar junctions with non-destructive dye penetrant testing (DPT).

Immediate Safety Action:
1. High risk of secondary stator core damage from broken bar centrifugal ejection: plan scheduled shutdown immediately.
2. Prohibit motor restarts or direct-on-line re-energization if broken rotor bars are suspected.
3. Verify insulation resistance and stator thermal indicators prior to any restart attempts.

Corrective Maintenance:
1. Remove motor from drive line and disassemble rotor into a clean workshop environment.
2. Inspect rotor cage for severed bars, loose lamination punchings, and end-ring fractures.
3. For fabricated copper rotors: replace damaged bars and TIG/laser re-braze into end rings with silver alloy.
4. For die-cast aluminum rotors: replace rotor assembly if two or more adjacent bars are fractured.
5. Dynamically balance rotor assembly to ISO 1940 Grade G2.5 prior to re-installation.

Preventive Maintenance:
1. Limit direct-on-line start cycles per hour according to NEMA MG-1 / IEC 60034 guidelines.
2. Utilize Variable Frequency Drive (VFD) soft-start acceleration profiles where feasible.
3. Monitor periodic MCSA decibel margins (sidebands within 35 dB of fundamental indicate urgent maintenance required).


==================================================
3. NORMAL MOTOR OPERATION (MOTOR_NORMAL)
==================================================

Fault Code:
MOTOR_NORMAL

Fault Description:
Motor electromechanical drive operates within standard thermal, mechanical,
and vibration specifications according to IEC 60034-1 and ISO 10816-3.

Severity:
NORMAL / LOW

Operating Baseline Thresholds:
- Thermal Gradient: Uniform thermal dissipation across housing (< 4°C differential).
- Hotspot Temperature: Below insulation class thermal rating (Class F: max 105°C rise).
- Vibration Severity: ISO 10816-3 Zone A/B (< 2.3 mm/s RMS for medium machinery).
- Acoustic Level: Normal continuous hum without periodic pulsing or modulated rumble.

Routine Maintenance Actions:
1. Maintain regular infrared condition monitoring schedule (quarterly baseline comparison).
2. Ensure cooling fins and fan cowl remain clear of dirt, dust, and oily debris.
3. Check terminal box connection tightness and cable gland integrity.
4. Monitor ambient temperature and ventilation airflow around the motor body.
